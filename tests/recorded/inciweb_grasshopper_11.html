Austin and Grasshopper Fires Incident Update 09-30-2026

Austin Fire, Grasshopper Fire
Publication Type: News - 09/30/2026 - 09:11

Grasshopper Fire Location: 
14 miles SW of Dufur, OR

Acres Burned: 93,971 acres
Containment: 98% 
Start Date: July 23, 2026
Cause: Lightning

Total Personnel: 761
Hand Crews: 7
Dozers: 5
Engines: 20
Heavy Equipment: 44

Austin Fire Location:
25 miles SE of Estacada, OR

Acres Burned: 12,884 acres
Containment: 98%
Start Date: August 19, 2026
Cause: Human

Public Information: 541-982-5802
Hours: 8 AM – 8 PM
Email: 2026.grasshopper@firenet.gov

This is the final daily update for the Austin and Grasshopper Fires from California Interagency Incident Management Team 2. Management of the fires will transition to Type 3 incident management teams at 1800 on Thursday, October 1. Future messaging about the Austin and Grasshopper Fires will be distributed by the Mt. Hood National Forest. CAIMT2 is grateful for the successful collaboration with Mt. Hood National Forest and Oregon Division of Forestry. The partnerships, leadership, and strong interagency coordination were essential to safely and efficiently achieving fire management objectives. Together, critical suppression repair was conducted efficiently and with a shared commitment to restoring the landscape and addressing resource concerns. The unified efforts in planning, operations, and resource sharing enabled swift responses to emerging needs and ensured the best possible outcomes for surrounding communities and lands. We appreciate the dedication, professionalism, and support each agency provided. It was a privilege to work alongside teams so committed to public safety and to one another. Thank you for your contributions to the success of the multi-fire response efforts.

Suppression repair work is being conducted in the Grasshopper and Austin Fire areas. Forest Road 44 is busy as trucks move slash and excavators repair sites that were used to park vehicles and store supplies. Felling modules completed hazard tree mitigation in several areas of the fires and equipment is now redistributing or hauling away logs. Chippers continue creating and dispersing mulch, excavators are repairing dozer line, and salvaged hazard trees are being hauled away from the fire area. Resource Advisors are guiding crews to effectively redistribute vegetation across landscape and seed more dozer line in the Rocky Butte area.

In the north section of the Grasshopper Fire, crews repaired a road that crosses a fish culvert along Ramsey Creek, which was damaged by suppression operations. When the fire was actively growing, crews had to improve an unused road to be used as an escape route for firefighters working in the area. Before the fire began, this area was under construction to improve habitat connectivity for steelhead trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) and other native aquatic species. Biologists and engineers from the Mt. Hood National Forest advised fire managers and heavy equipment operators to restore the area to its pre-fire condition, which included redistributing materials and recontouring roads.

Weather: A high-pressure system is heading northwest across the fires, bringing the last chance of precipitation for the next week. Temperatures are expected to rise, and the area will dry out. The forecast calls for slight wind and highs in the mid-60s.
